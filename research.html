Research

Numerical modelling of ice-sheet and earth systems

My research pairs methods-driven model development with scientific question-driven applications.

1. Ice-sheet – climate interactions on glacial cycle timescales

This work couples ice-sheet, sea-level and climate models to disentangle the individual sea-level contributions of the four paleo ice sheets over the last 21,000 years. We use large ensembles of simulations, representing uncertainty in forcing and model dynamics, to investigate how distinct ice-sheet configurations may be consistent with the sea-level record.

2. Open, reproducible research workflows

Large model runs are only useful if others can check and build on them. I develop automated, documented, version-controlled pipelines for running and post-processing simulations on shared HPC clusters, with an emphasis on workflows that a new student or collaborator can pick up without months of onboarding.

3. Glacier model emulation and calibration

My PhD work developed emulators of a popular subglacial hydrology model that are are up to 100,000 times faster than solving the numerical model. The emulators were used in a Bayesian framework to calibrate model parameters and reduce uncertainty in model predictions.

Simulations that couple the emulators with an ice-flow model add to existing evidence that modellling choices, such as the representation of basal hydrology and friction, strongly influence model predictions for the coming 100 and 300 years.

Background

How I got here

I completed my PhD at Simon Fraser University (2026), working on statistical emulation of subglacial hydrology models, supervised by Dr. Gwenn Flowers and Dr. Derek Bingham. Before that, I developed a model for supraglacial hydrology or the Greenland ablation zone at the University of Waterloo, supervised by Dr. Christine Dow. My research has been supported by the Natural Sciences and Engineering Research Council of Canada, the Government of Ontario, and Simon Fraser University. A full history is on my CV.
